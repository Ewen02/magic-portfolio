## Why RAG has become essential

**RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** is today the go-to approach for plugging an LLM into private data without retraining the model. Rather than stuffing everything into the context — costly and limited — you retrieve only the relevant passages and inject them at generation time.

In theory it's simple. In production, the quality of a RAG pipeline comes down to the details: how you split documents, how you rank results, and how you avoid paying twice for the same computation. Here are the choices that made the difference on [CorpusAI](/work/corpusai).

## 1. Parsing: it all starts with the data

A RAG pipeline is only as good as its ingestion. Before even thinking about embeddings, you need to cleanly extract text from heterogeneous formats:

- **PDF**: the classic trap, between columns, tables, and scanned PDFs
- **DOCX, Markdown, TXT, CSV, HTML**: each with its own structural quirks

The goal isn't merely to extract raw text, but to **preserve the structure** (headings, lists, sections) that will serve as a signal at chunking time.

## 2. Parent-child chunking: the key to relevance

This is the most underestimated step. Chunk too fine, and you lose context. Too large, and you dilute relevance and inflate costs.

The **parent-child** strategy resolves this dilemma:

- You index short **child chunks** (~150 tokens) for precise search
- You return the larger **parent chunk** (~512 tokens) surrounding the child to the LLM

You win on both fronts: retrieval precision *and* the richness of the context provided to the model. Token counting is done with `tiktoken` to stay aligned with the model's tokenizer.

## 3. Embeddings and vector store

For vectorization, OpenAI's `text-embedding-3-small` offers an excellent quality/price ratio (1536 dimensions). Cost tip: the **Matryoshka** property of these embeddings lets you truncate to 512 dimensions with minimal quality loss — less storage, faster search.

On the storage side, **Qdrant** stands out: HNSW index, cosine similarity, native metadata filtering, and simple cloud deployment.

## 4. Hybrid reranking: semantic + lexical

Vector search alone sometimes misses exact matches (proper nouns, references, codes). The solution: a **hybrid reranking** that combines two signals.

- **60% semantic** (vector similarity) for meaning
- **40% lexical** (BM25) for exact keyword matches

This blend captures both intent and literal precision. It's often what separates a "demo" RAG from a RAG that holds up in production.

## 5. Caching: performance and cost

Recomputing the same embeddings over and over is pure waste. A **Redis cache** on embeddings (7-day TTL) drastically reduces latency and the API bill on repeated requests.

## Overall architecture

The whole thing fits in a **Turborepo monorepo**:

- **Frontend**: Next.js 15 with token-by-token streaming (SSE)
- **Backend**: NestJS 11 for the API and orchestration
- **Asynchronous jobs**: BullMQ for heavy ingestion (parsing, embeddings) in the background
- **Storage**: Qdrant (vectors) + Redis (cache) + PostgreSQL (metadata)

Ingesting a document must never block the user: you acknowledge receipt, process it in a queue, and notify when it's ready.

## Key takeaways

1. **Chunking is the most profitable investment** — before optimizing the model, optimize the splitting.
2. **Hybrid retrieval beats pure semantic** in most real-world cases.
3. **Cite your sources with a confidence score**: transparency is what makes an AI assistant trustworthy.
4. **Handle ingestion asynchronously**: the user experience should never wait on a pipeline.
